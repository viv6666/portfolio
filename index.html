Engineering Philosophy

Building Products
That Ship.

Electronics and Communication Engineering graduate from SRMIST Chennai, now beginning a career in Electronics Manufacturing Services as a Graduate Engineer Trainee under Syrma SGS's Future Bets Program.

My interest sits at the point where a design becomes a manufacturable product — NPI, DFM/DFA, PCB assembly, and the testing and quality discipline that separates a prototype from something that ships at volume. I'm building fluency in industrial automation, SMT processes, and failure analysis alongside the embedded and PCB design work I already know well.

During my internship at SIEMAS Technologies, I engineered a production-grade dual-redundant industrial controller from scratch — 97% uptime under hardware failure simulations and 2-second automatic failover. That's the engineering standard I'm carrying into manufacturing.

Design for Manufacture
A board that can't be assembled at volume isn't finished. DFM and DFA thinking starts at schematic, not at the production line.
Fault-First Thinking
Every system I design accounts for failure modes before deployment. Redundancy and failure analysis are architectural, not an afterthought.
Hardware ↔ Process Unity
PCB design, firmware, and the manufacturing process behind them are one problem. I work across all three to eliminate integration failures at the source.
Continuous Improvement
Lean thinking applied to engineering: reduce iteration time, cut wiring complexity, and treat every test cycle as a chance to tighten the process.
